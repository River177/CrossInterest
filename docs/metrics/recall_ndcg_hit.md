# Recall, NDCG, and Hit: Metric Definitions and Computation

This document defines the `Recall@K`, `NDCG@K`, `Hit@K`, and `MRR@20`
metrics used in the current novel-topic prediction experiments.

The implementation is located at:

```text
src/new_interest/metrics.py
```

The evaluation data are the grouped Dev10K datasets:

```text
data/training/sft_experiments/eval/
├── behavior_only/dev_10k_grouped.parquet
└── interest_only/dev_10k_grouped.parquet
```

## 1. Evaluation Data

### 1.1 One prediction per impression

The Dev set is grouped by `impression_id`. Each impression contains:

- one user history;
- one ranked list generated by the model;
- one or more relevant novel topics.

For example:

```text
Relevant topic set G = {Golf, Cars & Driving}

Model ranking P =
1. American Football
2. Golf
3. Music
4. Cars & Driving
...
```

Because one impression may have multiple relevant novel topics, ordinary
single-label accuracy is insufficient.

### 1.2 Number of relevant topics in Dev10K

| Relevant topics per impression | Impressions |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8,410 |
| 2 | 1,195 |
| 3 | 283 |
| 4 | 73 |
| 5 | 21 |
| 6 | 13 |
| 7 | 1 |
| 8 | 3 |
| 9 | 1 |
| **Total** | **10,000** |

Therefore, 84.1% of impressions have one relevant topic, while 15.9% have
multiple relevant topics.

## 2. Notation

For user history \(u\):

- \(G_u\) is the set of clicked new interests;
- \(|G_u|\) is the number of clicked interests;
- \(P_u=(p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_{20})\) is the model ranking in descending score order;
- \(P_u@K=(p_1,\ldots,p_K)\) is the first K predictions.

The current evaluation first generates 32 beam paths, deduplicates them by canonical topic category, and retains up to 20 ranked categories.

## 3. Recall@K

### 3.1 Formula

\[
\mathrm{Recall@K}(u)
=
\frac{|P_u@K \cap G_u|}{|G_u|}
\]

Recall@K measures the proportion of all relevant topics retrieved in the Top-K predictions.

### 3.2 Full recall is not required to receive credit

Assume:

```text
G = {Golf, Cars & Driving, Personal Finance}
```

Then:

| Relevant topics retrieved | Recall@K |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | \(1/3=0.3333\) |
| 2 | \(2/3=0.6667\) |
| 3 | \(3/3=1\) |

Recall is therefore a fractional score. It does not require retrieving all
relevant topics before assigning a nonzero score.

### 3.3 Example

```text
Relevant topics:
{Golf, Cars & Driving}

Top-5:
1. American Football
2. Golf
3. Music
4. Television & Streaming
5. Personal Finance
```

Only `Golf` is retrieved:

\[
\mathrm{Recall@5}=\frac{1}{2}=0.5
\]

If `Cars & Driving` appears at rank 12:

\[
\mathrm{Recall@10}=0.5
\]

\[
\mathrm{Recall@20}=1.0
\]

## 4. Hit@K

### 4.1 Formula

\[
\mathrm{Hit@K}(u)
=
\begin{cases}
1, & |P_u@K \cap G_u|>0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]

Hit@K measures whether the Top-K list contains at least one relevant topic.

### 4.2 Difference from Recall@K

Assume an impression has three relevant topics:

| Relevant topics retrieved | Hit@K | Recall@K |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 1 | \(1/3\) |
| 2 | 1 | \(2/3\) |
| 3 | 1 | 1 |

Hit@K does not distinguish between retrieving one relevant topic and
retrieving all relevant topics. Recall@K does.

### 4.3 Single-relevant-topic case

If \(|G_u|=1\), then:

\[
\mathrm{Hit@K}(u)=\mathrm{Recall@K}(u)
\]

The number of retrieved relevant topics can only be zero or one.

Because some impressions in Dev10K have multiple relevant topics, the
dataset-level values generally satisfy:

\[
\mathrm{Hit@K}\geq\mathrm{Recall@K}
\]

## 5. DCG@K

### 5.1 Binary relevance

The experiments use binary relevance:

\[
\mathrm{rel}_i
=
\begin{cases}
1, & p_i\in G_u \\
0, & p_i\notin G_u
\end{cases}
\]

### 5.2 Formula

\[
\mathrm{DCG@K}(u)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{K}
\frac{\mathrm{rel}_i}{\log_2(i+1)}
\]

A relevant topic contributes more when it appears near the top of the
ranking.

| Rank \(i\) | Gain for a relevant topic |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | \(1/\log_2(2)=1.0000\) |
| 2 | \(1/\log_2(3)=0.6309\) |
| 3 | \(1/\log_2(4)=0.5000\) |
| 5 | \(1/\log_2(6)=0.3869\) |
| 10 | \(1/\log_2(11)=0.2891\) |
| 20 | \(1/\log_2(21)=0.2277\) |

## 6. IDCG@K

IDCG is the maximum possible DCG for an impression.

If \(m=|G_u|\), the ideal ranking places the
\(\min(K,m)\) relevant topics in the first positions:

\[
\mathrm{IDCG@K}(u)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{\min(K,|G_u|)}
\frac{1}{\log_2(i+1)}
\]

For an impression with three relevant topics:

\[
\mathrm{IDCG@5}
=
1+\frac{1}{\log_2(3)}+\frac{1}{\log_2(4)}
\]

\[
=1+0.6309+0.5=2.1309
\]

## 7. NDCG@K

### 7.1 Formula

\[
\mathrm{NDCG@K}(u)
=
\frac{\mathrm{DCG@K}(u)}
{\mathrm{IDCG@K}(u)}
\]

Its range is:

\[
0\leq\mathrm{NDCG@K}\leq1
\]

NDCG accounts for both:

1. how many relevant topics are retrieved;
2. where those relevant topics appear in the ranking.

### 7.2 Single-relevant-topic example

Suppose `Cars & Driving` is the only relevant topic.

If it is ranked first:

\[
\mathrm{NDCG@20}=1
\]

If it is ranked 14th:

\[
\mathrm{NDCG@20}
=
\frac{1/\log_2(15)}{1}
\approx0.2560
\]

If it does not appear in the Top-20:

\[
\mathrm{NDCG@20}=0
\]

### 7.3 Multiple-relevant-topic example

Suppose:

```text
G = {Golf, Cars & Driving}
```

The model ranks `Golf` second and `Cars & Driving` fourth:

\[
\mathrm{DCG@5}
=
\frac{1}{\log_2(3)}
+
\frac{1}{\log_2(5)}
\]

\[
\approx0.6309+0.4307=1.0616
\]

The ideal ranks are one and two:

\[
\mathrm{IDCG@5}
=
1+\frac{1}{\log_2(3)}
\approx1.6309
\]

\[
\mathrm{NDCG@5}
\approx
\frac{1.0616}{1.6309}
=0.6509
\]

Recall@5 is:

\[
\mathrm{Recall@5}=\frac{2}{2}=1
\]

This example shows that Recall can be perfect while NDCG remains below one
because the relevant topics are not ranked ideally.

## 8. NDCG@1, Recall@1, and Hit@1

### 8.1 NDCG@1

NDCG@1 only checks whether the first prediction is relevant:

\[
\mathrm{NDCG@1}
=
\begin{cases}
1, & p_1\in G_u \\
0, & p_1\notin G_u
\end{cases}
\]

### 8.2 Hit@1

Hit@1 also checks whether the first prediction is relevant:

\[
\mathrm{Hit@1}=\mathrm{NDCG@1}
\]

### 8.3 Recall@1

\[
\mathrm{Recall@1}
=
\frac{|\{p_1\}\cap G_u|}{|G_u|}
\]

If there are two relevant topics and the first prediction retrieves one:

```text
NDCG@1  = 1
Hit@1   = 1
Recall@1 = 1/2 = 0.5
```

Therefore, on multi-relevant-topic data:

\[
\mathrm{NDCG@1}
=
\mathrm{Hit@1}
\geq
\mathrm{Recall@1}
\]

All three metrics are identical when each impression has exactly one
relevant topic.

## 9. MRR@20

Let \(r_u\) be the rank of the first relevant topic:

\[
\mathrm{RR@20}(u)
=
\begin{cases}
\frac{1}{r_u}, & r_u\leq20 \\
0, & \text{no relevant topic in the Top-20}
\end{cases}
\]

\[
\mathrm{MRR@20}
=
\frac{1}{|U|}
\sum_{u\in U}\mathrm{RR@20}(u)
\]

MRR only considers the first relevant topic. It does not reward retrieving
additional relevant topics after the first hit.

## 10. Macro-Averaging Across the Dataset

Each metric is computed separately for every impression and then averaged
over all 10,000 impressions:

\[
\mathrm{Metric}
=
\frac{1}{|U|}
\sum_{u\in U}\mathrm{Metric}(u)
\]

Every impression receives the same weight. Impressions with more relevant
topics do not receive greater weight.

## 11. Random Baselines

### 11.1 Uniform random ranking over all 110 categories

Using the actual multi-topic distribution in Dev10K, the theoretical
expectations are:

| Baseline | Recall@1 | Recall@5 | Recall@10 | Recall@20 | NDCG@1 | NDCG@5 | NDCG@10 | NDCG@20 | Hit@20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Random over 110 | 0.91% | 4.55% | 9.09% | 18.18% | 1.11% | 2.80% | 4.32% | 6.69% | 21.22% |

The expected Recall is:

\[
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Recall@K}]
=
\frac{K}{110}
\]

### 11.2 Random ranking after excluding historical topics

For each impression, previously observed unique history topics are removed
from the 110-category space before producing a random ranking. The average
remaining space in Dev10K is 98.86 categories:

| Baseline | Recall@1 | Recall@5 | Recall@10 | Recall@20 | NDCG@1 | NDCG@5 | NDCG@10 | NDCG@20 | Hit@20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Random excluding history | 1.02% | 5.09% | 10.18% | 20.36% | 1.24% | 3.14% | 4.84% | 7.50% | 23.67% |

This baseline better reflects the constraint that a predicted novel topic
must not already appear in the user history.

## 12. Interpreting a Current Experiment Result

For Llama Interest 22K:

```text
Recall@20 = 43.29%
NDCG@20  = 17.81%
Hit@20   = 47.86%
```

Interpretation:

- on average, the model retrieves 43.29% of each impression's relevant topic
  set within the Top-20;
- after applying rank discount and ideal-ranking normalization, the mean
  NDCG@20 is 17.81%;
- 47.86% of impressions have at least one relevant topic in the Top-20.

Hit@20 is higher than Recall@20 because retrieving one topic from a
multi-topic relevant set gives Hit@20 a full score of one, while Recall@20
only receives partial credit.
